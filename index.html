The simple “sell a little high, buy a little low” tool

You do not guess which stock will go up next. You split your money in half. When one half grows, you sell a little of it and buy more of the other half. That is the whole trick.

What this idea is, in 3 steps

1. Split your money. Put $50,000 in Tesla and $50,000 in cash. Or Tesla and gold. Two buckets. Even split.
2. Wait for prices to move. One bucket will usually grow more than the other. Now you are no longer 50/50.
3. Rebalance. That just means: sell some of the winner and buy some of the loser until you are 50/50 again. Then repeat.

Fancy names you can ignore: people call this Shannon’s Demon or a constant-rebalanced portfolio. Same idea.

First, watch a pretend Tesla example

Tesla goes up 100%, then back down 50%. If you never touch it, Tesla ends where it started. Watch what rebalancing does.

Start. You have $100,000. $50,000 in Tesla. $50,000 sitting in cash.

Day 1. Tesla doubles. Tesla side is now $100,000. Cash is still $50,000. Total: $150,000.

You rebalance. You sell $25,000 of Tesla and park that $25,000 as cash. Now each side is $75,000.

Day 2. Tesla falls back to its old price (down 50%). The Tesla side becomes $37,500. Cash is still $75,000.

You now have $112,500.

Tesla itself went nowhere. You still made $12,500 because you sold some when it was high and bought it back when it was cheap.

What the words mean

Rebalance = sell some of whatever went up, buy some of whatever went down, so the two piles are even again.

Buy and hold = buy once and never sell. You just let the pile drift.

Multiple = how many times your money grew. 2x means $100,000 became $200,000.

Worst drop = the biggest fall from a peak to a low along the way. If you hate watching accounts crash, smaller is better.

Why this can make money = you automatically sell high and buy low. You do not need to know the future.

Why this can lose money vs just holding the winner = if one stock keeps going up for years, you keep selling the winner. You will have a smoother ride, but you may end with less money.
